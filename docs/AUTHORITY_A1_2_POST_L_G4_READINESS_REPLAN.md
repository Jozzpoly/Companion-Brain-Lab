## 2. What G4 is responsible for

G4 answers a narrower question than general social intelligence:

> **Does this physically admissible companion future respect asymmetric player agency well enough to remain a viable teammate action?**

Initial cooperation concerns:

- unnecessary head-on pressure against an Owner-directed player future;
- claiming/crossing the player's likely physical flow when an alternative can yield;
- blocking a stationary player or constrained passage;
- failure to release after the player-companion conflict clears;
- preferring a yielding alternative over a conflicting alternative when both remain otherwise viable.

G4 is **not** responsible for:

- defining relationship meaning;
- deciding final objective utility;
- route topology;
- smoothing/temporal embodiment;
- broad crowd etiquette;
- final runtime command authority.

## 4. Do not begin with a comfort radius

The old cooperation stack used a comfort envelope and weighted penalties. That can be useful later, but importing a fixed expansion now would hide an unproven social parameter inside the first G4 gate.

The first G4 slices should instead exploit facts we already possess:

- actual player and companion trajectories in the same physics;
- hard-body/contact evidence;
- relative geometry over time;
- explicit player-flow direction when the player intervention has meaningful velocity;
- stationary occupancy when it does not;
- alternative candidate futures.

This is sufficient to falsify the most damaging teammate failures — persistent head-on pressure, body-path crossing/blocking and refusal to yield — before deciding how much non-contact personal space feels natural.

A future comfort/clearance layer may be justified by Owner tests, but it must remain soft cooperation evidence and must never redefine hard World reachability.

## 6. A1.2n — asymmetric cooperation policy / yield preference

Only after A1.2m evidence qualifies.

The first policy should be deliberately comparative and bounded. It should not attempt to solve every social movement problem.

Core principle:

> **The player does not owe reciprocal avoidance to the companion.**

Initial policy questions:

- if candidate A creates persistent reciprocal pressure/contact under an Owner-directed future and candidate B remains physically viable while yielding, prefer B;
- if a candidate crosses/claims the player's body-flow while another candidate avoids that claim without creating a worse hard-safety state, prefer the yielding candidate;
- if the player future is stationary, do not fabricate lane priority; evaluate blocking/approach/egress instead;
- if all candidates conflict, report unresolved/forced-contention rather than pretending one is cooperative;
- if a pre-existing conflict is being resolved, do not penalize the egress action merely because contact still exists transiently.

This first policy should produce **cooperation preference evidence**, not the global A1 winner.

Possible result classes:

- `COOPERATIVELY_CLEAR`
- `YIELD_PREFERRED_OVER_CONFLICT`
- `EGRESS_PREFERRED`
- `FORCED_CONTENTION_NO_CLEAR_ALTERNATIVE`
- `UNRESOLVED_PLAYER_AGENCY`

These names are provisional until executable falsifiers justify them.

No scalar "cooperation score" should be introduced unless pairwise/lexicographic evidence proves insufficient.

Required falsifiers:

1. head-on: an otherwise legal yielding/hold alternative beats the persistent pressure candidate;
2. same-direction parallel: no unnecessary yield is demanded;
3. cross-front: a non-crossing alternative beats needless body-flow claim;
4. stationary player: blocking/approach is judged without invented forward direction;
5. egress from existing contact is not mistaken for new aggression;
6. no clear alternative -> explicit forced contention, not false PASS;
7. candidate family labels do not affect the result when physical trajectories are swapped;
8. player/companion roles are intentionally asymmetric — swapping roles is **not** required to preserve policy.
