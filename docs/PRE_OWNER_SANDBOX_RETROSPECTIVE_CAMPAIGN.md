## 1. Why this review exists

The project has accumulated a large amount of technically strong work in a very short period:

- physical-world apparatus;
- relational positioning;
- routing and local locomotion;
- temporal motion realization;
- robustness/recovery;
- causal workbench;
- CCC shadow coordination;
- A0 causal evidence;
- A1/A1.2 counterfactual and decision research;
- semantic provenance;
- Owner-Sandbox preparation and participant-first experiments.

That volume creates a serious epistemic risk:

> the project may have become increasingly capable of inspecting and qualifying movement while leaving the actual live teammate much closer to the old baseline than the repository's sophistication suggests.

Before spending Owner attention on an Owner Sandbox, the project must therefore answer a more basic question:

> **What companion have we actually built, what parts are genuinely valuable, what parts are research apparatus, what parts are historical debt, and what important teammate capabilities remain missing despite the apparent depth of the repo?**

This review is not a documentation cleanup and not a ritual before a predetermined OS-PREP-5 launch.

A legitimate outcome is that the current candidate is ready enough for Owner evidence.

An equally legitimate outcome is that the project must first simplify, repair, replace or add one missing capability before an Owner Sandbox would be high-value.

---

## 2. Owner intent being protected

The original project target remains stronger than “good movement AI”.

The companion should increasingly be experienced as a **competent partner present in the same causal situation as the player**:

- useful without babysitting;
- predictable enough to coordinate with;
- capable of meaningful initiative;
- corrigible/directable at low attention cost;
- physically grounded;
- readable through behavior;
- eventually able to participate in combat preparation, combat, commands and one-to-few companion cooperation;
- supported by a strong local live brain rather than reduced to LLM prompting.

Movement is the first collaboration language, not the product.

The review must explicitly reject any conclusion that silently substitutes:

- smooth motion for intention;
- collision avoidance for cooperation;
- a research frontier for a decision;
- deterministic tie-breaking for personality;
- green tests for teammate feel;
- debug explainability for behavioral readability;
- repository complexity for intelligence.

## 3. Evidence hierarchy for the retrospective

Use, in order:

1. direct Owner intent / direct Owner feedback;
2. live repository code and exact runtime authority;
3. qualified runtime/browser evidence;
4. participant-first artifacts;
5. controlled Twin/metamorphic evidence;
6. historical plans and qualification documents;
7. hypotheses inferred from architecture or code shape.

Historical plans do not receive authority because they were detailed.

A test result proves only its exercised contract.

A mechanism receives no product credit merely because it exists in the repository.

## 4. First reconstructed chronology

### Phase A — S0 physical cooperation apparatus

Strong result:

- World/Rapier boundary became trustworthy enough to expose real actor contact, obstruction and constrained-space failure.

Important limit:

- apparatus PASS was explicitly not movement-quality or teammate-quality PASS.

### Phase B — S1 relational positioning

Important Owner-evidenced failure:

- a reasonable endpoint around the player was insufficient;
- doorway behavior exposed missing traversal feasibility, constrained-space cooperation and progress/recovery.

Correct lesson:

> relationship intent and movement competence are separable problems.

### Phase C — S2/S3 movement intelligence

Material success:

- whole-body traversal evidence;
- route as guidance rather than direct motor authority;
- omnidirectional local candidate movement;
- pillar and doorway physical competence.

Open issue preserved at the time:

- the relationship objective remained the legacy eight-slot point model.

### Phase D — S4 natural motion

Material success:

- bounded temporal realization;
- smoother arcs and more embodied motion without losing basic pillar/doorway competence.

Critical warning:

- temporal realization can make discontinuous or bad upstream decisions look intentional.

Later evidence repeatedly confirmed this warning.

### Phase E — R1 robustness / Foundation

Owner browser failures invalidated earlier mechanical confidence.

Durable gains include:

- hard legality vs comfort separation;
- egress semantics;
- post-World progress/recovery;
- final-command hard safety;
- runtime fault containment;
- stronger causal evidence.

Foundation eventually became a credible **rework-ready substrate**, not a completed companion.

### Phase F — CCC behavior forensics

Important Owner signal:

- first weak spontaneous sense of “aliveness”.

Critical falsification:

- CCC shadow had zero movement authority;
- therefore the aliveness signal came from the existing movement stack, not new coordination cognition.

Useful candidate primitives:

- multiple temporal scales;
- history-dependent body/action state;
- non-monotonic movement;
- direction+speed choice;
- temporal body realization.

Living-error warnings:

- slot-label continuity != spatial intention continuity;
- deterministic side bias;
- route tie bias;
- stale semantic frames;
- progress episode identity coupled to symbolic labels;
- smooth motor realization can counterfeit coherent intention.

### Phase G — A0 / A1 / A1.2

A0 produced valuable causal/provenance seams.

A1/A1.2 then grew a very large body of:

- player-future hypotheses;
- query-only same-physics rehearsals;
- candidate command generation;
- hard safety;
- player-agency trajectories;
- pairwise cooperation evidence;
- relationship quality and PACE evidence;
- structured frontiers;
- robustness and metamorphic audits;
- observer non-interference;
- bounded P2 one-step intervention.

But the dominant A1 runtime remained **zero/pass-through authority**.

Current repository shape reflects this strongly:

- `src/coordination`: 184 files / ~1.67 MB;
- A1 subset: 152 files / ~1.46 MB;
- A1 test files: 102;
- `src/brain`: 39 files / ~259 KB.

This does not make A1 useless.

It does mean that repository sophistication must not be confused with live companion sophistication.

### Phase H — OS-PREP

OS-PREP correctly reacted to the research/apparatus drift by returning to participant-visible phenomena.

High-value findings include:

- PH-01: smooth arcs can hide ~2.27 m objective relocation;
- PH-02: stale retained semantic policy can materially prolong close contact;
- PH-03: live slow pace can be better than shadow PACE suggests;
- PH-04: open-space cooperation can emerge without a right-of-way FSM;
- PH-05: doorway separation -> reacquisition -> settle can work without special chokepoint policy;
- PH-08: useful multi-beat continuity can coexist with discontinuous upstream intent;
- PH-07: deterministic router tie-breaking can become unearned topology preference.

OS-PREP-4 then reused this evidence as a participant-first synthesis.

That was useful, but it also created a new risk:

> “we have enough pre-Owner evidence” may have become a conclusion about evidence apparatus readiness rather than a conclusion about the live companion's breadth.

The present retrospective exists to challenge that transition.

## 5. Provisional high-risk findings

These are **review hypotheses**, not final verdicts.

### R-H1 — live teammate cognition may still be shallow

In the current ordinary `SPATIAL / NATURAL / A1 OFF` runtime, the authoritative chain is approximately:

`legacy eight-slot relationship objective`
→ `static route`
→ `S3/R1 local spatial scoring`
→ `NATURAL refinement/continuity`
→ `World/Rapier`
→ `progress/recovery`.

A1 does not choose the companion action.

CCC does not choose the companion action.

Therefore the active teammate may still be fundamentally:

> a sophisticated, robust, smoothed relational locomotion system with limited semantic memory and recovery,

rather than the broader local live brain imagined in the original project intent.

This must be tested, not assumed.

### R-H2 — some of the best “life” may come from dynamical embodiment rather than intelligence

That is not necessarily bad.

History-dependent movement, multiple timescales and physical continuity may be genuine ingredients of believable agency.

But the project repeatedly observed that the same machinery can also make:

- target teleports;
- stale objectives;
- deterministic tie-bias;
- delayed correction

look plausibly intentional.

The retrospective must identify which visible gains survive when accidental pseudo-agency is removed.

### R-H3 — the project may have over-invested in decision research before earning the decision problem

A1.2 accumulated a very large capability to compare candidate futures and audit decision evidence while no production selector existed.

Repeated replans themselves warned against:

- scalar selectors;
- future voting;
- tangent pulses;
- arbitrary tie-breaks;
- automatic singleton execution;
- promotion from internal evidence alone.

This suggests the research discipline was strong, but the research target may have remained too close to “which velocity?” for too long.

### R-H4 — OS-PREP improved methodology but may still be movement-bounded

OS-PREP restored participant-first discipline and found real product-facing pathologies.

However most tested phenomena remain variations of:

- follow/relationship;
- pace;
- crossing;
- doorway;
- route choice;
- continuity.

This may be appropriate for the first embodied layer.

It may also mean that an Owner Sandbox today mostly evaluates **locomotion relationship feel**, not the broader companion dream.

The value of that sandbox depends on whether we explicitly want that narrow question.

### R-H5 — packaging readiness and product readiness are separate

The provisional `437cd...` candidate has strong provenance and participant-surface mechanics.

That demonstrates packaging competence.

It does not establish that this is the right organism to spend Owner judgement on next.

## 6. Review tracks

The retrospective proceeds through seven tracks.

### T1 — North-star continuity audit

For every major stage:

- what product question was it supposed to answer?
- what Owner feedback caused it?
- what did it actually prove?
- did the next stage follow the evidence or the apparatus?
- did any temporary representation quietly become long-lived authority?

### T2 — Live authority anatomy

Build the exact current authoritative call graph.

Classify every major subsystem as:

- live authority;
- safety/competence support;
- passive observation;
- explicit bounded intervention;
- historical donor;
- dead/legacy baggage.

This track must make it impossible to mistake “implemented” for “active”.

### T3 — Evidence/claim reconciliation

Audit major PASS/FAIL claims against their actual evidence class:

- contract;
- scenario;
- browser/runtime;
- participant;
- Owner.

Look specifically for:

- claims that later Owner evidence invalidated;
- green suites that lacked product coverage;
- participant interpretations that may have been biased by experiment design;
- reused evidence whose scope changed.

### T4 — Mechanism value / pathology audit

For each active live mechanism ask:

- what useful behavior does it produce?
- which failures does it cause?
- is it conceptually durable or only scaffolding?
- does a simpler mechanism explain the benefit?
- would deleting/replacing it remove an accidental pseudo-agency source?

Priority mechanisms:

- eight-slot relationship objective;
- retained semantic frame behavior;
- static router deterministic ties;
- S3 local candidate scorer;
- NATURAL refinement and continuity;
- progress/recovery;
- hard-vs-comfort repair.

### T5 — Missing-brain audit

Compare current live capabilities against the original teammate problem.

Explicitly inspect absence or weakness of:

- situational responsibility;
- short-lived intention/action identity;
- meaningful initiative;
- command interpretation;
- pre-combat readiness;
- threat/role reasoning;
- attention/focus;
- local goals beyond maintaining relation;
- low-cost local memory beyond movement history;
- player correction/corrigibility;
- higher cognition seam as a real teammate input.

Do not implement these during the audit.

### T6 — Research-apparatus cost audit

Identify:

- A1/CCC modules that are durable donor substrate;
- modules that only support old hypotheses;
- tests that defend important invariants;
- tests that defend abandoned research geometry;
- browser harness cost / CI latency;
- documentation authority debt;
- branch/PR lineage debt.

The goal is not cleanup yet.

The goal is to understand how much cognitive/maintenance load the current corpus imposes on future work.

### T7 — Owner-evidence readiness decision

Only after T1–T6 decide:

- what exact question an Owner Sandbox should answer;
- whether the current organism is the right stimulus;
- whether known pathologies are acceptable evidence or invalidate the run;
- whether one small pre-Sandbox rework is justified;
- whether OS-PREP-5 packaging should be reused, changed or discarded.
